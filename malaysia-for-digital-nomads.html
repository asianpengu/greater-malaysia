The visa: DE Rantau Nomad Pass

Who runs it: the Malaysia Digital Economy Corporation (MDEC).

Income needed: at least USD24,000/year from foreign sources for digital/tech professionals (some non-tech professional roles: USD60,000). One of the lowest bars of any nomad visa.

Tax: 0% Malaysian tax on foreign-sourced income.

Where: Peninsular Malaysia. How long: a renewable pass (typically 3–24 months, extendable in 12-month steps).

How to apply: fully online via MDEC; fee about RM1,060 for the main applicant, plus ~RM500 per dependent. Since June 2024 it also covers founders, CEOs, and professionals like accountants, lawyers and writers.

Always confirm the current terms on the official MDEC DE Rantau site before applying.

The internet is fast (don't believe the old numbers)

Crowdsourced nomad sites often list Malaysian internet as "slow." The official data says otherwise. Per Ookla's Speedtest Global Index (2026), Malaysia's median mobile internet is ~154 Mbps (25th in the world) and median fixed broadband is ~170 Mbps. Fibre is widely available in KL and Penang, and 5G coverage is extensive. Cafés and co-working spaces are plentiful.

Common questions

Does Malaysia have a digital nomad visa?

Yes — the DE Rantau Nomad Pass by MDEC. It needs at least USD24,000/year in foreign income (USD60,000 for some non-tech roles) and gives 0% tax on foreign-sourced income in Peninsular Malaysia.

How much money do you need?

USD24,000/year in foreign income for digital/tech professionals — one of the lowest thresholds anywhere. The application fee is about RM1,060 for the main applicant.

Is the internet fast in Malaysia?

Yes — about 154 Mbps median mobile and 170 Mbps median fixed broadband as of 2026 (Ookla). Old crowdsourced figures understate it badly.

Do digital nomads pay tax in Malaysia?

The DE Rantau pass gives 0% Malaysian tax on foreign-sourced income. Home-country obligations still apply.
